Question: Have a look at the ‘Who is responsible for governance?’ section, followed by the ‘Corporate governance framework’ section. Can you identify who in your organisation performs these roles? Do you have a person or function that specialises in governance?

RIDDOR – The Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013
Health and Safety Executive (2013). Reporting accidents and incidents at work.

RIDDOR requires companies to report certain health and safety events to the Health and Safety Executive (HSE). Their website provides a brief guide to RIDDOR.


You can access the downloadable guide here: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg453.htm

Question: Referring to the list of ‘Specified injuries to workers’ on page 2 and that of ‘Reportable occupational diseases’ on page 3, do you know what your organisation would do to manage any of these situations in line with its health and safety policy and procedures?

Question: With reference to pages 4 and 5, consider how you would report any incidents or situations relating to your area of responsibility. What is the procedure for reporting outside of normal working hours, if applicable? How does this align with your organisational policies on health and safety?

Introduction to Data Protection for SMEs
Information Commissioner’s Office (N.D.). An introduction to data protection for small businesses and sole traders. Webinar.

In the lesson, we introduced data protection legislation, with a focus on the General Data Protection Regulation – GDPR. This webinar, produced by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), gives small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) an overview of data protection to help them check that they are compliant with data protection and GDPR.


You can access the webinar here: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/whats-new/e-learning/#intro

Question: At just over three minutes in, the webinar mentions the six lawful bases for holding personal data, which organisations must comply with. Can you think of an example from your organisation that satisfies each lawful basis?

Question: At around 13 minutes in, the webinar discusses the right that people have to request the information that is being held about them – also known as a ‘subject access request’ (SAR). Is there a situation related to your organisation in which an individual may make a SAR?

Understanding the Equality Act 2010
Gov.UK (2015). Equality Act 2010: Guidance.

In the lesson, we considered the laws and regulations that impact the work of managers. The Equality Act 2010 is the key piece of legislation that protects people from discrimination in the workplace and wider society.

This resource provides information and guidance on the Equality Act 2010, including age discrimination and the ‘public sector Equality Duty’ requirements.


You can access the website here: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/equality-act-2010-guidance

Question: The overview section shows that the Equality Act 2010 legally protects people from discrimination in the workplace and in wider society. Click on the ‘who is protected’ link to see the ‘Discrimination: your rights’ page. How does this information relate to the needs and requirements of your team or the departments you are responsible for?

Question 2: Continuing on the ‘Discrimination: your rights’ page, click on the ‘Discrimination at work’ section in the contents list. There you’ll find a ‘preventing discrimination at work’ link, which leads to the ‘Employers: preventing discrimination’ page. Could your organisation’s processes in relation to any of the three examples listed be improved upon in your area of responsibility to support anti-discriminatory practices?
