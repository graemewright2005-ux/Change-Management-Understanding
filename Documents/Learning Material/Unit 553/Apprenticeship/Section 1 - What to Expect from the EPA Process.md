Section 1 - What to Expect from the EPA Process
 
   
Welcome to the first section in the apprenticeship End Point Assessment (EPA) preparation unit. In this section, we will introduce you to the EPA process so that you understand what is involved and can start to prepare for it. Your apprenticeship will be concluded once you successfully complete the EPA.

An EPA is an assessment at the end of your apprenticeship. It assesses you against the knowledge, skills and behaviours (KSBs) in the Skills England occupational standard.

The learning and assessments you have carried out whilst on-programme during your apprenticeship have allowed you to demonstrate the KSBs required for the Operations Manager occupational standard. You will also have demonstrated the duties required as part of the standard as you conducted your workplace role.

Therefore, the EPA is your opportunity to show an independent assessor how well you can carry out the occupation you have been trained for against these standards.

 
   
EPA Gateway
Before you are able to undertake your EPA, your employer – along with you and your learning provider – will need to agree that you are ready. This means that you must have finished and passed all aspects of your on-programme learning, completed your on-programme project and any pre-gateway work. Additionally, your employer must confirm that you are consistently working at or above the requirements of the Level 5 Operations Manager occupational standard.

You may also need to have completed an English and Mathematics qualification as part of your apprenticeship, which you will evidence at this point as well. If you are unsure whether these requirements apply to you, check with your learning provider or apprenticeship tutor/assessor.

The EPA ‘gateway’ is the point at which the End Point Assessment Organisation (EPAO) – an external body from your learning provider – checks and confirms that you have met any necessary requirements before you start your final assessment.

You will only enter the gateway when your employer agrees you are ready, you have completed the on-programme learning, and you have been on-programme for at least the minimum time required for the occupational standard.

The typical length of on-programme time for the Operations Manager apprenticeship is 24 months. The EPA typically lasts 5 months.

The gateway for your EPA requires you to have:

If applicable, achieved English and Mathematics qualifications in line with the apprenticeship funding rules
Completed the on-programme project
Compiled and submitted a portfolio of evidence demonstrating the required KSBs
See Section 2, ‘Preparing for the Written Project Report’ and Section 4, ‘Preparing a Portfolio of Evidence’, for guidelines. You will deliver your project during your learning on-programme and write up the associated report post-gateway. The portfolio must be compiled whilst on-programme and checked to ensure that it covers the KSBs required for the professional discussion to be ready to submit at the gateway.

The gateway is the point where you are preparing to demonstrate your competence against the Operations Manager occupational standard for the summative assessment at EPA. ‘Summative assessment’ means that you will be evaluated on what you know and can do against the criteria using the agreed formal assessment processes to achieve your apprenticeship.

Whilst your tutor or assessor will guide you and make sure that you have the resources and time to prepare and undertake the EPA, you will be responsible for managing your own time and planning how you will demonstrate your competence to the required level.

In addition, your tutor or assessor will inform you of how to apply for your EPA and develop an EPA plan, but they will not provide any teaching, learning or mentoring once you have left the on-programme stage. There will be paperwork and processes that you must complete for your EPA and these must be submitted in a specific order, so it’s important to be clear and organised from the outset.

 
   
End Point Assessment Phase
Once you have passed through the gateway and completed the actions required, you will then enter the EPA phase of your apprenticeship journey. As you know, the EPA is your opportunity to showcase to an independent EPAO what you have learned and what you are able to do. The EPA aims to test your understanding and application of the KSBs for the Operations Manager occupational standard and ensure that you can perform the duties required.

The process involves you undertaking a number of activities to demonstrate your competence according to the criteria. The criteria are split into different formal assessment methods. You can access Skills England’s EPA requirements to find out which criteria are needed for each particular assessment method. Understanding how these are categorised will help to ensure that you focus on the specific criteria for the correct assessment method.

The Operations Manager occupational standard has two assessment methods that are used to assess competence:

Assessment Method 1: Written project report with presentation and questions
Assessment Method 2: Professional discussion underpinned by a portfolio of evidence
We’ll now look at the requirements of each method.

 
   
Assessment Method 1: Written Project Report with Presentation and Questions
This method requires you to have identified a suitable project, relevant to your occupational standard, and delivered it as a piece of work during your on-programme learning.

Once you have entered the gateway, you will then be required to complete a written report of 4,000 words and prepare a 20-minute presentation, including 40 minutes of question time, to demonstrate how you have met the criteria linked to your project report and those required for this assessment method.

 
   
Assessment Method 2: Professional Discussion Underpinned by a Portfolio of Evidence
The professional discussion consists of you and the Independent End Point Assessor (IEPA) having a formal, two-way discussion about the work that you have been doing. It’s an opportunity for you to demonstrate the KSBs required for this assessment method and you should aim to showcase these in the best way possible.

The discussion is underpinned by a portfolio of evidence, which you will have previously compiled using examples from the workplace relevant to the criteria being tested by this assessment method. The portfolio will have been submitted to the assessor at the gateway stage and you will have access to it during the discussion so that you can refer to it throughout and evidence the points you want to make.

You can find out more about how to prepare for your professional discussion in Section 5 of this unit and how to prepare a portfolio in Section 4.

 
   
Conducting the Assessment
The assessment methods can be delivered in any order and will be carried out in a suitable working environment, such as an EPAO’s office or your employer’s premises. You should ensure that the assessment is carried out in a quiet room, free from distractions and interruptions. Increasingly, EPAs for the Operations Manager standard are conducted online, via video-conferencing software.

If you have any special needs or requirements that would be helpful for the independent assessor to know, make sure that you mention these to your tutor or assessor at the beginning of the EPA process so that they can help to plan the right assessment environment for you. If you have a disability, a physical or mental health condition or other specific considerations, you may be able to have a reasonable adjustment made to take this into account.

It is also worth noting that the two assessment methods (the presentation and professional discussion) are often held on the same day. If this is the case, you will be provided with an opportunity to have a break and prepare yourself between the assessments. Use this time wisely to rest and perhaps bring some refreshments. You should also build in time to prepare any resources that you need before the next assessment and ensure that you are focused and prepared for the set of specific criteria required for that particular assessment method.

After you have undertaken all the parts of your EPA, the EPAO will assess your work against the grading criteria and send the results to your learning provider.

 
   
EPA Grading
Performance in the EPA determines your overall grade and you will be required to pass both assessment methods – and meet all ‘pass’ descriptors – to achieve an overall ‘pass’ grade.

‘Distinctions’ are awarded to apprentices who achieve a ‘distinction’ level grading in both assessment methods.

If you fail one or both assessment methods, you will be awarded a ‘referred’ grading if CMI is your EPAO.

For further details of the grading descriptors – and what distinguishes ‘pass’ and ‘distinction’ levels – see the ‘Grading’ section of Skills England’s EPA plan. Where CMI is the EPAO, grading descriptors can be found in ‘Appendix 3’ of the CMI’s ‘L5 Operations Manager apprenticeship handbook’.

What happens when you pass your EPA?

Once you pass your EPA, you will be awarded with a certificate from Skills England showing that you have met the requirements for the Operations Manager level 5 apprenticeship standard and passed your EPA.

The results will need to be verified by the EPAO, so it is unlikely that you will be informed of your results on the day of assessment, and the official result and certificate may take some time to be issued to you.

Successful achievement of the apprenticeship also aligns with CMI’s membership pathway.

What happens if you do not pass your EPA?

If you do not meet the required standard during your assessment, don‘t worry, anything can happen on the day and retakes and resits are not uncommon the first time around. You will be given specific feedback on what elements did not meet the requirements and you can resit or retake the particular assessment method required at your employer’s discretion.

In the case of resits or retakes, ask your tutor or assessor for guidance on this immediately after the assessment. If you don’t require further learning to meet the standard, you should be able to arrange to resit one or both of the elements of the EPA relatively quickly. Resits are typically required to be completed within two months of your original assessment date. If further training is required, this can be completed first – the retake must usually be taken within a six-month period.

You will also be required to respond to a different set of questions in your assessment, and if the assessment that you did not pass was part of the presentation with questions, you will also be given a new topic to present. In most cases, the highest grade awarded for resits and retakes will be a ‘pass’.

In all cases, the most important thing is to not panic or focus on the negatives. You will have progressed so much throughout your apprenticeship and this is just one further hurdle to overcome before you reach your goal. Have a look at the sections in this unit again when preparing for your assessment for your resit or retake.

 
   
Developing an EPA Plan
Before you begin the EPA planning process, it’s a good idea to familiarise yourself with the difference between the specific assessment methods used in your EPA and how to approach them. Have a look at Ofqual’s ‘What to expect from your EPA’ information on the different assessment methods for guidance.

You should also pay attention to the ‘command verbs’. These are verbs – words describing actions – that guide you as to what you are expected to do. For example, you may be asked to ‘demonstrate’ an activity, which is different from being asked to ‘prepare’ an activity.

According to the UK Government’s ‘Apprentice guide to assessment’, to prepare for your EPA you should:

Review the assessment plan to familiarise yourself with the assessment methods so that you know what to expect
Make use of all practice materials shared with you to understand the different assessment methods
Make sure that any work required has been completed and submitted
Speak with other apprentices or past apprentices to get tips for assessment
In advance of the EPA you should confirm:

The date and time of your EPA
Where your EPA is being held and how you will get there if it’s being delivered in person
Equipment you will need, such as pens or a calculator
Technology you will need to use
When and how you will find out your results
It’s a good idea to practise in advance of your assessment if you are using any technology that you’re unfamiliar with. This is particularly important if your EPA is being conducted online or you require access to your portfolio from a different digital platform than you are used to.

A key part of the EPA plan will be communicating with your tutor or assessor to check that the process is progressing and that all elements have been submitted at the correct time. Another is the preparation of resources that you will need and the approach you are aiming to take – Sections 2–4 of this unit can help with this by demonstrating ways to approach each part of the EPA methods.

You should refine the performance elements of the assessment beforehand, ideally, by rehearsing your presentation and your discussion topics, along with consideration as to how you will respond to questions on the day. You may be able to practise this with your tutor or assessor as part of a mock assessment. If this is not possible, consider practising with a trusted observer who can help you to understand the elements that work well in showcasing your competence, as well as highlight any aspects that you could improve upon. Doing this will also help you to feel more prepared on the day.

Lastly, enjoy the process – you have worked hard to get here. Completing your on-programme apprenticeship and reaching your EPA is a huge achievement. Consider the EPA as a good opportunity to showcase what you know and your abilities as a manager of operations.
