Section 3 - Preparing for the Presentation
 
   
As we saw in Section 2, one of the End Point Assessment (EPA) methods for the Operations Manager apprenticeship standard is a written project report, and one component of this is delivering a presentation on the project you delivered on-programme, as well as answering related questions during the session.

The presentation supports the written report by providing an opportunity for the assessor to verify that you completed the activity and seek clarification where required. It is also designed to assess the knowledge, skills and behaviours (KSBs) that you may not have had the opportunity to demonstrate through your project report to the level of competence needed for the grading descriptors.

However, it is still important that missing or weak criteria are kept to a minimum and avoided altogether where possible to ensure that both the report and the presentation are as comprehensive as they can be in the areas they focus on.

The presentation forms one part of two components that make up the assessment methods for the following group themes:

Project scope and planning
Project implementation
Evaluations and recommendations
Within these group themes, the presentation should specifically cover:

An analysis of the internal and external factors that impacted the choice of project
A critical evaluation of the project activity, justifying the reason for the project
Detail about the impact the project has had since it was completed
By now, you are likely to have carried out your on-programme project and once you have passed through the ‘gateway’ you will be ready to write up and present your report.

Examples of projects include:

Introduction of a new process, service or product to an operational area
Identification and implementation of a change plan to improve efficiency or make a direct saving (e.g. percentage decrease in direct costs or a reduction in headcount)
Implementation of a performance improvement plan for the operational area, which required significant collaboration with wider stakeholders
Evaluation of an internal communications project that involved communicating change to different stakeholders
Note: This list may not include your actual project theme. It provides examples of topic areas, so please ensure that you have checked the topic and scope of your project for suitability with your tutor or assessor when you come to deliver it or when submitting it for the gateway.

Where CMI is the End Point Assessment Organisation (EPAO), the presentation and questions will last at least 60 minutes – with the presentation typically lasting 20 minutes and the Q&A the remaining 40 minutes. The independent assessor will ask you at least six questions.

Content
For this component of the ‘Written project report with presentation and questions’ assessment method, you are required to draw on your experience from the work-based project you delivered on-programme and provide a project report written post-gateway. You will also need to create a presentation based on your project scope and planning and implementation methodology, and outline your post-project evaluations and recommendations.

Although you should structure the presentation in the recommended format, your time should be spent focusing on the content and ensuring that you are meeting the assessment criteria. You will not be assessed on your presentation skills or the use of high-tech options when creating your presentation. However, it is a good idea to make your presentation look as professional as possible.

As discussed, the presentation should cover:

An analysis of the internal and external factors that impacted the choice of project
A critical evaluation of the project activity, justifying the reason for the project
Detail about the impact the project has had since it was completed
The presentation is designed to supplement the written project report and is a further opportunity for you to demonstrate the KSBs mapped to this assessment method to the highest available grade.

When preparing your presentation, you should look at the grading descriptors for the themes being assessed through this method – details of which can be found in your EPAO’s assessment grading guidelines. In the case of CMI as the EPAO, these can be found in ‘Appendix 3’ of the CMI’s ‘L5 Operations manager apprenticeship handbook’. Your EPAO may also have other guidance documents, such as the CMI’s ‘EPA toolkit’.

Let’s look at each of the elements above in detail.

When it comes to analysing internal and external factors, you should consider what evidence you have available that could help to justify the reason for the project. You may have conducted a SWOT, PESTLE or force-field analysis to help you identify internal and external drivers that impacted your choice of project. There may be emails, internal data or research that explains how and why the project came about. The key thing here is to ensure that you undertook the analysis and can explain what you did. You will be expected to show that you examined the internal and external aspects thoroughly. You can find out more about what the term ‘analyse’ means in CMI’s command verb definitions.

When critically evaluating and justifying the reason for the project, it is likely that you will have carried out or been involved in producing a business case for the project. It may have involved some budgeting or you might have conducted a cost–benefit analysis to check viability before you started. You could refer back to your initial scoping document or project initiation document (PID) for useful inclusions for your presentation. ‘Critically evaluate’ goes further than simply explaining the strengths and weaknesses or relative merit of pursuing the project, it considers the “strength of the evidence based on the validity of the method of evidence compilation” – as viewed from different perspectives. Refer to the CMI command verb glossary for further details.

When it comes to detailing the impact of the project, setting proper benchmarks and having metrics in place at the beginning will be key here. Furthermore, if you have clear objectives in mind from the outset, this will help when you assess whether you have achieved what you set out to, especially if your objectives are ‘SMART’ – ‘specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound’. If you are able to include quantitative data, such as cost savings or productivity timeline improvements, this can be very powerful, as can qualitative evidence like customer comments and stakeholder feedback.

Ensure that your content at all times focuses on how you have demonstrated the three key assessment criteria group themes for this assessment method as stated at the beginning of this section. You should also make sure that you have shown the part that you specifically played – your responsibilities and tasks and what you learned from the process overall.

 
   
Checking and Practice
Once you have developed your presentation, double-check that it meets as many of the KSBs within the three grouping themes as possible and consider how it complements your written report, as well as how you will fill any gaps. You can speak to your tutor or assessor for guidance on what to do if there are areas that you don’t think you have adequately covered.

Check the presentation for spelling and grammar accuracy and that the information is presented logically and looks professional for the assessor and audience.

It’s at this stage that you may want to practise your presentation in case you want to make any changes based on the delivery rather than the content. Rehearse the presentation for timings to make sure that it doesn’t run too short or too long of the 20 minutes required. You will be submitting your presentation to the independent assessor ahead of the assessment and at the same time as your written report, so you should ensure that it presents well in practice and make any necessary changes before submission.

 
   
Submission and Delivery
The next stage is to send your presentation to the EPAO along with your 4,000 word written project report. Your tutor or assessor will be able to guide you on how to do this but submission is usually via an EPA portal where you will be required to upload your presentation slides and any supporting materials by the end of week four of the EPA period.

You will prepare to deliver the presentation you have submitted as your assessment on the agreed assessment date. On the day, the independent assessor will watch and listen to your presentation before asking a series of structured questions related to the criteria it corresponds to and the written project report. The presentation and the written project report make up the two components of this assessment method and both will be marked holistically with an overarching grading decided on by the assessor. During your presentation, the assessor may write notes and you might notice that they use a purpose-built bank of assessment questions aimed at ensuring fairness of opportunity between apprentices.

You may be nervous on the day – this is to be expected. Remember that the independent assessor is keen to hear about you and your work and how it relates to the Operations Manager standard. You are well placed to explain what you do in your role so try to relax into your presentation so that you can fully explain what you do in the best possible way. The presentation, along with the responses you provide to the questions, is your opportunity to bring your work to life and demonstrate what you have learned throughout your apprenticeship.
