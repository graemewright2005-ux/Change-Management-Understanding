Section 5 - Preparing for the Professional Discussion
 
   
The second End Point Assessment (EPA) method for the Operations Manager apprenticeship standard is a professional discussion. Here, an Independent End Point Assessor (IEPA) will have a formal, two-way discussion with you about the work that you have been doing.

It’s an opportunity for you to demonstrate the knowledge, skills and behaviours (KSBs) required for this assessment method. You should aim to showcase these in the best way possible.

Portfolio of evidence

The professional discussion is usually supported by another assessment component – in this case, the ‘portfolio of evidence’ containing real examples of your work. You will have compiled your portfolio of evidence during your on-programme training and will then submit it to the End Point Assessment Organisation (EPAO) at the ‘gateway’ point. This allows the independent assessor to review it before the assessment day.

Although the assessor won’t assess it as part of your grade, it will provide them with some prior context for you and your work.

During the professional discussion, you and the assessor will be able to access and refer to the portfolio. The independent assessor can also ask you questions about it as part of the discussion, giving you the opportunity to bring your work to life through your descriptions.

Refer to Section 4 for more information and guidance on the portfolio of evidence.

Your tutor or assessor will be able to guide you on the specific elements required for your professional discussion. If CMI is your EPAO, you will be required to demonstrate the following KSBs grouped into four key themes:

Managing and leading a team
Organisational governance
Operational planning
Stakeholder relationships
These will be evidenced using your portfolio of evidence and in your professional discussion.

 
   
Structure
The professional discussion must be structured to give you the opportunity to demonstrate the KSBs mapped to this assessment method and to reach the highest available grade where possible. The IEPA will help you to do this by asking questions based on the criteria they are looking for you to demonstrate.

Ideally, the discussion should feel more like a structured conversation led by you, rather than an interview in which you simply respond to questions posed. You should plan to lead the conversation by presenting information from your portfolio that you feel best meets the criteria and bringing your work to life.

You should also anticipate what questions may be asked based on the criteria and consider how you might respond to these.

 
   
During the Discussion
In addition to the discussion, the IEPA may also focus on what they have reviewed in your portfolio and ask you questions about this. Be sure that you know your portfolio well, and how it links to the points made during your discussion as well as, crucially, where it all relates to the relevant KSBs. You should consider the key areas you are aiming to cover and try to prepare responses to a range of questions as well as how to best showcase the relevant KSBs using examples of your past experience and the work in the portfolio.

It might be in your nature to be modest about your work or feel that you should only present examples of resounding achievements. However, it is helpful to be confident about your abilities but honest in your approach. Ensure that you can explain how you have met the criteria with the work that you have done but without exaggerating the role you played or the results achieved. It’s also okay to give reflections of what you might do even more effectively next time or explain how you dealt with a situation in which things did not go quite as well as you had planned.

Remember that you can refer to your portfolio to support your discussion. It is also worth preparing your answers for questions in areas that you feel have been harder to evidence and may appear weaker than others in the portfolio. This kind of preparation can be a good opportunity for you to plug any gaps through discussion, and it’s likely that the assessor will hone in on areas where they need more evidence against certain criteria than they can see in your submitted work.

The assessor will ask questions in a natural way as part of your discussion, and they may also use a purpose-built bank of structured assessment questions.

Where CMI is the EPAO, the professional discussion will typically last 60 minutes, but this can be increased by up to 10% if the IEPA deems it necessary.

The assessor must ask at least six questions, and follow-up questions can be used if clarification is required.

CMI provides at least two weeks’ notice of the date of the discussion and they require your portfolio at least two weeks ahead of this to review before the discussion takes place.

There is a set timeline of events to be followed in a particular order. This further supports why being organised and having an effective EPA plan is an advisable approach.

 
   
STARR Technique
Lastly, it’s a good idea to prepare examples of what you might say to demonstrate your competence and practise how you will deliver these fluently throughout the discussion. You should also check and ensure that what you select to discuss fully covers all the assessment criteria.

Having a stock of responses to questions you may be asked could be helpful, along with preparing the structure for how you wish to lead the discussion. For example, if you are aiming to demonstrate criteria for K20 and S4 – ‘Analyses performance data for individuals and teams to identify areas for improvement, continuous development requirements and learning needs’ – in the ‘Managing and leading a team’ theme group, you should prepare an approach that allows the assessor to understand how you fulfilled all of these elements, ideally supporting this with examples from your work in your portfolio.
