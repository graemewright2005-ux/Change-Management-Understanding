Section 4 - Preparing a Portfolio of Evidence
 
   
The second End Point Assessment (EPA) method for the Operations Manager apprenticeship standard is a professional discussion with an Independent End Point Assessor (IEPA). This will be supported by a ‘portfolio of evidence’.

According to CMI, a portfolio of evidence is a “collection of pieces of evidence, gathered together on-programme, that is used as the underpinning basis of an end-point assessment method”. These pieces of evidence will be referred to throughout the discussion.

Although the portfolio itself is not assessed and does not form part of your overall assessment grade, you should ensure that you have gathered the right types of evidence of your work so that you can refer to them in your discussion and meet the criteria required for this assessment method.

You will need to prepare the portfolio before you enter the ‘gateway’ – indeed, while you are on-programme – so that you can submit it as part of the gateway requirements.

The portfolio of evidence underpins the professional discussion and is based on the following group themes:

Managing and leading a team
Organisational governance
Operational planning
Stakeholder relationships
CMI considers there to be four key steps to gathering the evidence you need to build a showcase portfolio.

Collect: Gather relevant evidence for your portfolio as you work through your on-programme training
Select: Check that the evidence you have collected showcases your best work and focuses on your management and leadership skills
Connect: Present the evidence in a way that makes it clear to the independent assessor how it maps to the knowledge, skills and behaviours (KSBs) needed for the professional discussion
Reflect: Ensure that you know the evidence well and how you are going to use it to explain what you have learned or done
A ‘showcase portfolio’ should be the best skills-based and work-based examples of how your work meets the KSBs required for the professional discussion. It can take many forms, such as letters, feedback, ‘thank you’ emails, observations or reports that you have produced.

Consider what workplace evidence you have that can either be presented as a single item or activity, or a combination of two to three examples in a single piece of evidence that collectively meets one of the criteria. For example, a series of reports and data collected about a decision that you made.

It’s important that your work-based evidence is real and has not just been created as an attempt to meet the assessment criteria. It is also key to present a succinct ‘portfolio check’ to your End Point Assessment Organisation (EPAO) based on their portfolio size requirements.

 
   
Assessing Your Evidence
As well as combining evidence, consider whether any of it is ‘holistic’. This means that one piece of evidence can cover more than one area of the competencies required. Usually, the evidence will be grouped together into the themes of the assessment method and labelled or mapped to the KSBs to show what criteria you are showcasing it against. Your EPAO is likely to have recommended templates that you can use for this. Your tutor or assessor can also help.

You are also encouraged to ‘triangulate’ your evidence where possible. So, if you have a work-based example of an activity, you could also ask a manager to confirm that the activity has been approached in a certain way to provide behavioural evidence. This will also give context to the situation and learning to showcase the underpinning knowledge of your actions.

In addition, you should ensure that the evidence gathered has been checked to ensure that it does not breach any data protection, confidentiality or safeguarding policies or laws. Evidence containing sensitive information, such as customer details or company budgets, can have the identifying information redacted – or scored out – so that it can still be used as evidence.

Finally, it’s also important that the work-based evidence meets the ‘VACSR test’. VACSR stands for ‘valid’, ‘authentic’, ‘current’, ‘sufficient’ and ‘reliable’. You can find out more about the quality criteria that your EPAO expects by asking your tutor or assessor for guidance.
