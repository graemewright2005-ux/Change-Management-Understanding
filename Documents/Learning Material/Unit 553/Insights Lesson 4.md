Collaborating with Remote Team Members
To successfully implement the operational plan, it’s necessary to keep in touch regularly with your team members, and this can be a challenge if some, or all of your team work remotely.

This online article provides insight into how to collaborate effectively with remote employees.

Dhawan, E. and Chamorro-Premuzic, T. (2018) How to Collaborate Effectively If Your Team Is Remote, 27 February.


Access the article here: How to Collaborate Effectively If Your Team Is Remote (hbr.org).

Questions:

The article suggests a number of challenges when managing remote team members. What specific challenges do you currently face, and how could you overcome these challenges?
Three kinds of ‘distance’ are highlighted: physical, operational and affinity. What could you do to reduce ‘affinity distance’?
A number of best practice techniques are summarised. Which ones resonate with you the most/least?
Based on this reflection, what steps can you take to improve communication and collaboration with people working remotely?
Note, if you do not have any team members working remotely, the steps identified could be applied to other remote stakeholders e.g. those working within your operational supply chain. Identify one of these stakeholders, and consider how the above questions relate to them instead.

Coaching
The lesson explained the importance of supporting a culture of improvement by creating opportunities for learning.

This online article introduces a framework that can be used to develop team members through coaching.

Mind Tools. The GROW Model of Coaching and Mentoring, MindTools.


Access the article here: https://www.mindtools.com/an0fzpz/the-grow-model-of-coaching-and-mentoring

Questions:

The GROW model provides a framework to help to structure a coaching (or mentoring) session. How user-friendly is this model? For example, could you use it ‘as is’ to coach a member of your team? Or would you need to adapt it – and if so, how?
The article provides examples of questions you might ask at each step. What other open questions could you add to this list?
Identify a member of your team (or a colleague) who would benefit from coaching to improve a specific area of their performance. Conduct a coaching session using the GROW framework. When completed, reflect on the session and ask yourself the following questions: What went well? Not so well? What could I improve upon for next time? Based on this reflection what will you start doing, stop doing or continue to do?
Gap Analysis
Gap Analysis is used to measure the gap between planned and actual performance.

This article summarises five gap analysis tools that can be used to ‘close the gap’ between planned and actual operational performance.

Athuraliya, A. (2020) 5 Gap Analysis Tools to Identify and Close the Gaps in Your Business, 21 October.


Access the article here: https://creately.com/blog/strategy-and-planning/gap-analysis-tools/

Questions:

Which of the five different tools would be most appropriate to use in your operation, and why?
Which of the five different tools would be inappropriate to use in your operation, and why?
Select a specific problem area to focus on and apply one of the tools to this situation. How useful was this tool in identifying the gap, and identifying actions to resolve the issue?
