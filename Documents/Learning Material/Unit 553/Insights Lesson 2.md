Cost–Benefit Analysis
Cost–benefit analysis helps managers to evaluate the expected benefits and drawbacks of different plans to help determine which is the optimal approach.

This article provides a summary of the tool’s advantages, limitations and relevance.

Hayes, A. (2024). Cost–benefit analysis: How it's used, pros and cons. Investopedia.


Access the article here: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/cost-benefitanalysis.asp

Questions:

Follow the suggested steps to compare the costs and benefits of two different courses of action to achieve a specific objective. Based on this analysis, which option is the optimal plan?
How user-friendly did you find the suggested steps? What are the advantages and disadvantages of the process?
How could you adapt the step-by-step approach to be more suitable for use in your own organisation? For example, could you produce a template to carry out future cost benefit analyses?
Case Study: Zara’s Supply Chain Success Story
Zara, the Spanish fashion retailer, managed to reduce the time it takes for a garment to move from the design phase to retail from the six months’ industry average to two weeks.

This online article provides insight into how they successfully managed their supply chain.

Lee, H. (2019) How extreme agility put Zara ahead in fast fashion, 10 December.

Risk Treatments
A key aspect of operational planning is to identify possible risks and take proactive action to manage these.

This online article summarises the main ways to respond to risks, depending on their likelihood and impact.

UNECE (2016). Guidelines on risk management practices in statistical organizations.


Access the article here: https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/Risk%20Management%20Guideline%20-%203%20Draft%20FINAL_0.pdf

Questions:

The article identified four main ways to approach risk: Tolerate, Treat, Transfer and Terminate.

Do you use all four approaches in your operation?
What examples can you identify to illustrate each of these four approaches?
If you are not currently using any of the options, is this something that you would consider? If not, what is the reason for this decision? If yes, how would you take this approach forward?
A fifth option was also suggested: ‘Take the opportunity’. Would you ever take this approach? If so, identify an example when this approach could be appropriate. If not, do you need to reflect on this approach further?
