Business Performance Metrics
Key performance indicators - sometimes called metrics - are used to measure how the operational plan is progressing.

This online article provides insight into eleven business performance metrics that can be used to report on, and evaluate operational performance.

Williams, A. (2017) 11 Key Business Performance Metrics for Better Operations, Aliste Marketing, 4 April.


Access the article here: https://alistemarketing.com/blog/business-performance-metrics/

Questions:

Which of the eleven measures do you currently use to measure operational performance?
Of the eleven measures identified, which ones are the most important ones to focus on?
Considering your own organisation, what additional metrics would you add to the list, and why?
Lean Management
The lesson introduced the concept of continuous improvement, with specific reference to taking small, incremental steps.

This online article provides insight into how to continuously improve business processes through lean management.

Fotsch B., Case, J. (2017) Transforming Your Service Business With Lean Management, 7 March, Forbes.


Access the article here: https://www.forbes.com/sites/fotschcase/2017/03/07/transforming-your-service-business-with-lean-management/?sh=5060dd554e81

Questions:

The article states that “nearly every business process generates waste – muda in Japanese – meaning any activity that doesn’t provide value to the customer”. Consider one of the processes within your operation - are there any activities within this process that are not adding value? If so, what is your first step to address this ‘waste’?
A key aspect of Kaizen, which means “change for the better” in Japanese, is employee involvement. How do you encourage employees in your operation to get involved in suggesting and making improvements?
Based on the content of this article, would you consider your organisation to be a lean one? Provide reasons for your answer.
Performance Targets
An essential task of any operational planner is to set out the objectives detailing what the plan must accomplish.

This document summarises a ten step target setting process, based on a two-year research study carried out by Professor Mike Bourne and Dr. Monica Franco-Santos, Cranfield School of Management.

CGMA. (2012) Set the right performance targets: A 10-step target setting tool.


Access the pdf here: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/set-the-right-performance-targets-a-10-step-target-setting-tool

Questions:

Bourne and Franco-Santos’ research focused on setting performance targets in relation to sales; however, the key points can be applied to other business areas.

The research highlighted three common target setting issues. Do any of these apply to target setting within your operation?
Reflect on your operation, and work through each of the ten stages. How user friendly did you find this process? Could you implement this approach ‘as is’, or would you need to adapt it in some way?
